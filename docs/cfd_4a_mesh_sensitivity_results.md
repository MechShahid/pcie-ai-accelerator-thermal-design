# CFD-4A Mesh Sensitivity Results

## Purpose

CFD-4A was added as a refined-mesh repeat of the final CFD-3 ducted heatsink case.

The purpose of this case is to check whether the final 250 W forced-air cooling result remains thermally feasible when the mesh is refined.

This is a mesh-sensitivity check only. It is not a full mesh-independent validation.

---

## Baseline case

The baseline case is CFD-3.

| Quantity | CFD-3 baseline |
|---|---:|
| Heatsink geometry | 80 × 120 × 35 mm |
| Domain type | Ducted |
| Chip heat load | 250 W |
| Inlet air velocity | 5 m/s |
| Inlet air temperature | 25 °C |
| Maximum chip temperature | 83.52 °C |
| Average chip temperature | 78.77 °C |
| Outlet temperature | 35.96 °C |
| Pressure drop | 49.02 Pa |
| Energy balance error | approximately 0.36% |

CFD-3 passed the 85 °C maximum chip-temperature target with approximately 1.48 °C thermal margin.

---

## Refined mesh

The CFD-4A mesh was generated by refining the final CFD-3 mesh while keeping the same geometry, materials, heat load, and boundary conditions.

| Mesh quantity | CFD-3 baseline | CFD-4A refined mesh |
|---|---:|---:|
| Nodes | 79,689 | 191,534 |
| Elements | 187,932 | 511,566 |
| Maximum aspect ratio | 15.0 | 10.716 |
| Minimum element quality | 0.122 | 0.186 |
| Minimum orthogonal quality | 0.122 | 0.201 |

The refined mesh has approximately 2.7 times more elements than the CFD-3 baseline mesh.

The mesh-quality indicators improved in the refined case, with higher minimum element quality, higher minimum orthogonal quality, and lower maximum aspect ratio.

## Solver setup

The same solver setup was used as in CFD-3.

| Setting | Value |
|---|---:|
| Solver | Pressure-based |
| Energy equation | On |
| Viscous model | Laminar |
| Inlet velocity | 5 m/s |
| Inlet temperature | 298.15 K |
| Outlet pressure | 0 Pa gauge |

| Number of iterations | 700 |

The purpose was to isolate the effect of mesh refinement only.

## Residual behaviour

At 700 iterations, the residuals were:

| Residual | Value |
|---|---:|
| Continuity | approximately 2.04e-1 |
| x-velocity | approximately 6.03e-5 |
| y-velocity | approximately 5.24e-5 |
| z-velocity | approximately 9.52e-5 |
| Energy | approximately 1.02e-5 |

A small reversed-flow region was reported at the outlet, affecting approximately 0.5% of the outlet area.

The continuity residual remained higher than the default convergence target, similar to the previous CFD cases. However, the momentum and energy residuals were low, the heat balance was good, and the engineering quantities were stable enough for a portfolio-level mesh-sensitivity comparison.

## Heat balance

The air-side heat removal was estimated using:

```text
Q_air = m_dot cp ΔT
```

Using:

```text
m_dot = 0.02260125 kg/s
cp = 1006 J/kgK
ΔT = 309.12586 - 298.15 = 10.97586 K
```

gives:

```text
Q_air ≈ 249.5 W
```

For a 250 W chip heat load, this gives an energy balance error of approximately:

```text
Energy balance error ≈ 0.2%
```

This is a good heat-balance result for a first-pass conjugate heat-transfer CFD comparison.

## Interpretation

The refined mesh still predicts that the final ducted heatsink design passes the 85 °C maximum chip-temperature target.

The refined case gives:

```text
Maximum chip temperature = 78.11 °C
Target temperature = 85 °C
Thermal margin = 6.89 °C
```

The outlet temperature is almost unchanged compared with CFD-3, showing that the total heat removal is consistent between the two meshes.

The pressure drop increased from approximately 49.02 Pa to 56.36 Pa. This is reasonable because the refined mesh can resolve the ducted flow resistance differently.

However, the maximum chip temperature changed by approximately 5.4 °C. Therefore, this result should not be described as mesh independent.

The correct conclusion is that the refined mesh supports the thermal feasibility of the final design, but further refinement and local interface/near-wall resolution checks would be required before claiming mesh-independent CFD validation.

## Visualization note

For clarity, the CFD-4A temperature result is shown using solid component surfaces. A centre cut plane through the fin array can appear fragmented because it intersects many thin fins, air gaps, and solid-fluid interfaces.

An additional isometric solid-surface temperature image is included to show the refined-mesh thermal distribution across the heatsink fins, base, and chip region.

The pressure result is shown on a centre X-plane using a manual static-pressure range of 0 to 60 Pa. This range is consistent with the CFD-4A pressure drop of approximately 56.36 Pa.

---

## Conclusion

A refined-mesh repeat of the final CFD-3 ducted heatsink case was performed using approximately 511k elements, compared with approximately 188k elements in the baseline mesh.

The refined case predicted a maximum chip temperature of 78.11 °C and a pressure drop of 56.36 Pa, compared with 83.52 °C and 49.02 Pa in the baseline case.

The energy balance error was approximately 0.2%, and the outlet temperature remained almost unchanged.

The refined case therefore supports the thermal feasibility of the final ducted heatsink design. However, the approximately 5.4 °C change in maximum chip temperature means the result should be treated as a mesh-sensitivity check rather than a fully mesh-independent validation.
